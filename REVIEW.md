# Adversarial review — Cantó & García-Rodríguez (2026), "Progressivity and Redistribution of Wealth Taxation in Spain" (EQUALITAS WP-95)

**Process.** Five reviewer lenses (data/replicability, design, measurement, literature, framing) ran independently over the full paper and the authors' slides; every finding was then judged by three adversarial verifiers (paper-internal, external primary sources, author-advocate). Majority verdicts: **34 CONFIRMED, 5 PLAUSIBLE, 0 REFUTED** across 39 findings, plus a full 47-entry citation check (37 verified, 9 verified-with-errors, 1 not found). Full evidence, verifier reasoning, and likely author responses: `REVIEW-APPENDIX.md`. The paper is under review at *International Tax and Public Finance* (Research Square preprint rs-9834492, submitted 27 May 2026).

**Paper in one breath.** Using the AEAT/IEF/INE Panel de Hogares 2022 wave (1.05M sampled households / 2.97M people; weighted 44.9M individuals, common fiscal territory), the paper simulates the Spanish wealth tax (IP) and seven alternatives (Norway, Zurich, French IFI, €300k threshold, no regional rebates, both, Piketty) on a fixed population with no behavioral response. Current IP: 170,404 payers, €1,126M, zero short-run wealth-Gini effect (Table 3, p.21). Broad bases multiply revenue 14× (€15.9bn Piketty), finance a €1,680–2,103/child universal benefit cutting the disposable-income Gini 4.2–4.9% (1.6–1.8 points, Table 4, p.25), and cut the top-1% share up to 8pp over 100 mechanical years (Fig. 5). Their own decomposition attributes the gains to incidence (level effect), not progressivity — Kakwani/Suits *fall* under the broad designs they favor.

## Findings

### The baseline is not 2022 law (design/framing)
- **CONFIRMED** — **ITSGF mis-dated and wrongly excluded from "current law".** Ley 38/2022 (BOE, consolidated) entered into force 29 Dec 2022, accrues each 31 December → first tax year **2022**, the paper's own valuation date; extended indefinitely by RDL 8/2023. Paper says "applicable on 2023" / "for 2023–2024" (p.5, fn.2). Ejercicio-2022 ITSGF raised **€623M from 12,010 taxpayers (€555M = 89% Madrid)** ≈ **49% of Sim. 2's incremental €1,279.6M** — the flagship "remove regional rebates" reform is partly the status quo.
- **CONFIRMED** — **Reality has kept moving their way.** Madrid suspended its 100% rebate while the ITSGF stands (Ley 12/2023); AEAT's 2023 IP statistics: €1,926.3M collected (+€676.3M, 90.6% from Madrid's rebate elimination), ITSGF falling to ~€35M. Actual current-law revenue ≈ **€1.96bn vs the paper's €1.13bn "current design"** — a live natural experiment for validating Sim. 2, and a cautionary one: the ITSGF's €623M came in ~60% below the government's ~€1.5bn forecast.

### Static ceilings, priced by their own citations (literature)
- **CONFIRMED** — **Zero-behavior revenue is an upper bound, and the wedge is quantifiable.** Advani & Tarrant (2021): base falls 7–17% per 1pp → Sim. NO €15.5bn → **€12.8–14.4bn**. Mas-Montserrat et al. (2025, JPubE; their citation): −3.21% taxable wealth per 0.1pp ATR, **92.6% of avoidance via the IRPF–IP cap — which Sims 1–3 keep**. Brülhart et al. (2022, their citation): ≥43% reported-wealth response per 1pp = the upper bound. Erosion scales with rates, so it is not ranking-neutral — it actually *strengthens* their breadth-over-rates conclusion while deflating the headline euros.
- **CONFIRMED** — **"Moderate responses" (p.6) understates their own citations** (43%/1pp; Jakobsen long-run elasticity 1.15 at the top; Spanish avoidance cost 2.75× the 2011 revenue).
- **CONFIRMED** — **The single most relevant paper is missing and would help them**: Agrawal, Foremny & Martínez-Toledano (AEJ: Applied 2025) — Madrid's zero rate; mobility-induced PIT losses 6× direct wealth-tax losses; **Madrid itself would gain from a harmonized positive rate**. Also absent: Seim (2017), Londoño-Vélez & Ávila-Mahecha (2021), Summers–Sarin.
- **CONFIRMED** — **Norway benchmark:** Sim. NO raises 1.13% of Spanish GDP vs Norway's actual ~0.48–0.58%; the replication uses 2023 rates on 2022 data and omits the 1.1% top bracket, the >NOK 10M housing tranche, secondary dwellings at 95%, and the shares discount.

### Mechanical results needing a re-run (measurement)
- **CONFIRMED** — **The equivalized long-run Gini panel is mathematically impossible.** Under uniform growth the no-tax distribution is scale-invariant, and with their own tax concentration (C_t = 0.9744) the cumulative IP levy of 0.50% at T=25 can move the Gini ≤ ~0.17pt *downward*; Table 5 reports **+1.04pt upward**. The advocate verifier concedes the panel needs an audit. The "inequality rises for decades" narrative (p.27) should not survive as stated.
- **CONFIRMED** — **A 35% wealth-total contradiction.** Every printed t and t_s/r coheres on total wealth ≈ **€5.3T**; Table A.1's mean (€160,540 × 44.86M) implies **€7.2T**. EFF 2022 (mean €309k/household, BdE DO 2413) supports ~€5.5T — Table A.1's descriptives look wrong, and t_s/r seeds the whole long-run analysis.
- **CONFIRMED** — **The scenario base is not documented asset by asset.** Baseline IP taxes *declared* property values (p.9); Norway/Zurich use market-derived housing values (25%/70% of ×3.08 cadastral) — Zurich's housing base is ~2.16× the Spanish legal base before any rate applies, so part of the cross-design gap is revaluation, not "design breadth." Tables 1/A.2 label most other scenarios only "net wealth." The institutional section says pensions are exempt in Norway and Switzerland, while the methods never state how pension rights are identified or removed; business assets are observed only for IP filers, liabilities are not decomposed, and the asset-level bases for FR/Sims 1–3/Piketty remain unpublished.
- **CONFIRMED** — **Consistency sweep**: Table 3's net-wealth R–S column is silently ×1000 vs Table A.4; overlap matrix (Table A.8) has impossible cells (Piketty∩Sim.2 = 297,219 > Sim.2's 201,687; Sim.1∩Sim.2 = 72,186 < the forced 170,404); ratio 3.0838 vs 3.1125 on the same page; 7 vs 8 vs 9 scenarios; text's "950k households / 2.7M individuals" is the 2019 wave; France €800k vs €1.3M; Sim. 2's t = 0.04% (Table 3) vs 0.05% (Table 5).

### Framing vs their own tables (framing)
- **CONFIRMED** — **Abstract claims reforms "increase tax progressivity"; their Table 3 shows K and Suits falling** under NO/CH/Sim.1/Piketty and flat under Sim. 3 (K 0.2313 → 0.1912 NO). The true headline — progressivity and redistribution move in opposite directions across designs — is a better paper than the one the abstract sells.
- **CONFIRMED** — **Slide errors to fix before Friday**: "4–5 Gini points" is 4–5 *percent* (1.6–1.8 points); ">7pp, from ~15% to <8%" fails for Norway on every basis in the paper (−5.5pp, ends at 9.13% equivalized); the trilemma's "0% vs 1.3%" mixes wealth and income Ginis; the g-sweep "robustness" is near-vacuous by construction (g cancels from scale-invariant indices).
- **CONFIRMED** — **"Efficiency" in the abstract has no analysis behind it**; "robust empirical evidence" = one cross-section, no SEs, one uniform valuation ratio.

### Data and replicability (data)
- **CONFIRMED** — **The data are genuinely obtainable — the code is the binding constraint.** The PH is a joint INE–AEAT–IEF product (waves 2016–2023, M714 wealth-tax module), delivered **free to any researcher who signs the IEF protocol** — so outside replication is possible in principle. But there is no code, no parameter appendix; the Zurich design is fully unparameterized (statutory 0.3% top vs ~0.65% with Steuerfuss — a 2.17× ambiguity); EUROMOD's Spain model cannot simulate the IP ("Lack of data on wealth impedes simulation," JRC country report); PolicyEngine has no Spain model. Publishing parameter tables + code makes this fully replicable.
- **CONFIRMED** — **Never validated against AEAT** — yet it validates well: 170,404 payers / €1,125.58M vs official 185,101 / €1,250.0M (−8%/−10%). They should report this. Caveat: the PH shows only 4 weighted zero-quota filers vs 45,264 official (Madrid/Andalusia >€2M declarants largely invisible → Sim. 2's base partly unobserved wealth).
- **CONFIRMED** — **Silent foral exclusion**: weighted 44.86M = common fiscal territory (INE: Spain 47.43M − Basque 2.18M − Navarre 0.66M); "Basque," "Navarre," "foral" appear nowhere.
- **PLAUSIBLE** — Child-benefit divisor is 7.543M minors (identical in all 8 rows) vs ~8.2M under-18s nationally (~7.7M common territory) — per-child amounts modestly overstated; state the divisor.

### Checked and does not hold (don't raise)
Nothing was refuted outright, but soften these: the child-benefit divisor gap partly dissolves under the foral-territory scope (advocate's point); "DCE-M (2014) said abolish it" is true (verbatim "El impuesto sobre el patrimonio debe eliminarse") but the same authors' later work is reform-oriented — use only as a nuance on "the literature points to reform"; the gender narrative is statistically right, just small in absolute terms (−1 to −4% relative) — ask for relative reporting, don't call it wrong.

## Discussant questions (ranked by how much the answer changes the paper's conclusions)
1. **Valuation base per scenario** — which real-estate value enters each design's base (declared vs ×3.08 market)? Ask for the decomposition: Piketty run on the IP's declared-value base, separating revaluation from design. *Likely response: rankings/denominators always at market; statutory bases differ by design as intended — "valuation rules are part of design." Then the ask becomes: report the split.*
2. **The ITSGF and 2023 reality** — given first accrual 31 Dec 2022, €623M collected, Madrid's rebate suspension, and 2023 IP receipts of €1.93bn: will you relabel the baseline a pre-ITSGF counterfactual and use 2023 as an out-of-sample validation of Sim. 2? *Likely response: deliberate, disclosed exclusion for comparability; will fix the dating; 2023 validation is a good idea.*
3. **The equivalized long-run panel** — under uniform g, how can the equivalized Gini rise 1.04pt when the tax's maximum movement is 0.17pt? *Likely response: concede and audit; equivalization/re-ranking hypothesis fails their own C_t.*
4. **The 60% IRPF–IP cap in Sims 1–3/Piketty** — is it applied, and how much revenue does it remove, given Mas-Montserrat's 92.6% and Table A.8's 3,577 IP payers with zero Piketty liability? *Likely response: Spanish sims inherit it; will report its bite.*
5. **Behavioral bounds** — will you publish the static numbers alongside a 7–17%-erosion band (their own UK citation nets this out) and cite Agrawal et al., which strengthens their harmonization case? *Likely response: static-by-design, elasticities are policy-contingent; will add bounds.*
